# Extracting a Useful Method From Historical Material

## Start with an attributable claim

Record the specific passage or artifact, speaker or author, edition or timestamp,
and enough context to understand the claim. Distinguish an original statement,
a translation, a mirrored copy, a scholarly explanation, a publisher summary,
and a catalog record. Multiple copies of one source are not corroboration.

A framework is an interpretation. Explain the evidence that motivates it and
where the interpretation goes beyond that evidence. Do not manufacture a quote
to make a modern application feel authentic.

## Keep the unit of evidence small enough to inspect

Link the relevant local source card and name the passage or section that supports
the claim. A broad bibliography cannot support every sentence in a research note.
An index is useful for discovery; an unseen chapter is not evidence.

Retain source language, speaker boundaries, publication and capture dates,
rights notices, and truncation markers. Do not confuse public accessibility
with permission to redistribute or apply the project license to source text.

## Develop a bounded application

Describe the decision the method helps, the relevant observation or mechanism,
and a failure case. Preserve the user's objectives and constraints. A modern
application should be marked as such, and any hypothetical inputs should be
labeled. A historical example does not establish current facts.

Prefer one substantial, useful note over repeated topic-name substitution.
Remove demonstrated duplicated padding without discarding unique source content.
Keep contradictions, uncertain attribution, and changes over time visible.

## Review behavior as well as structure

Check whether a realistic request receives a useful answer without invented
facts, fake quotations, involuntary language changes, or unauthorized actions.
Document checks verify links and metadata; they cannot establish interpretive
truth or quality. Keep language choices in SKILL.md and maintain both README
versions together.
